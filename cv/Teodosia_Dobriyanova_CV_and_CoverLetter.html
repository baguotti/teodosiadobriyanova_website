Dear Guardian team,

I am delighted to be applying for the Digital Journalist Scheme – video, audio and pictures pathway. I am particularly drawn to this role as it merges my experience in video journalism and filmmaking with my ambition to learn more about the profession and expand my expertise.  

I was trained in ethnographic and documentary filmmaking where I first cut my teeth in factual storytelling and the ethical approaches and responsibilities around filming people and documenting their stories. What attracted me to documentaries in the first place was the connection to the world and to others that the camera facilitates, as well as the possibilities to tell the stories that feel important.

After graduation, I worked with the Multimedia Anthropology Lab at UCL as a video producer where I was part of a collaborative project that documented accounts of gender-based violence in Brazil’s Guarani-Kaiowá community. We worked with audio testimonies and video footage filmed by the participants. The project coincided with an installation I directed in Bulgaria which also focussed on the stories of survivors of violence, and merged audio with animations in an interactive web-based environment.

Between 2021 and 2026, I spent five years as a video journalist at Mashable where I worked across climate change and sustainability, tech, current events, culture, and entertainment. I was an all-round video producer pitching and researching stories, sourcing and conducting interviews, filming on location, and editing. I got to tell stories about floods in Sudan and flood adaptation in China, heatwaves in Europe and the UK, the wars in Ukraine and Gaza, online disinformation, the rise of online prediction markets, and more. Although I focussed predominantly on video journalism, I also pitched and wrote occasional articles. I will leave a link of sources to these stories at the end of my cover letter.

At Mashable, I worked on pre-existing video series but also developed my own, including Mashable Now, which aimed to focus on current events through Mashable’s tech and digital culture lens, and How to Change a City – an urban design series looking at ways to make cities more sustainable and equitable.

I developed a particular interest in climate change and came to realise that the topic is insufficiently covered in the media landscape of my country of origin, Bulgaria. I co-created an oline platform called Planet A where we tried to deliver climate change news and awareness to online audiences through accessible explainers in Bulgarian. Growing slowly but surely, we attracted local collaborators such as Greenpeace Bulgaria and Klimateka – a community of climate scientists who were trying to adopt their studies into easily digestible articles. The team behind Klimateka felt that they weren’t succeeding at abandoning the academic language, and so we worked with them to translate their findings in a way that would feel accessible to readers outside of the scientific community.

As I have no formal training in journalism, Mashable was my journalism school. I was mentored by some incredible editors and colleagues and learned a lot about the work. But I am strongly convinced that I would benefit immensely from a training scheme like this one, where I can receive structured training while at the same time also further develop my practical experience. As an avid Guardian reader, I am excited by the opportunity to learn from some of the UK’s most talented journalists, and work across video, podcasts, pictures, and more. As someone with a broad range of interests and an interdisciplinary background, it is important to me to work in a dynamic and varied environment where a multimedia approach is encouraged.

Sincerely yours,

Teodosia Dobriyanova
